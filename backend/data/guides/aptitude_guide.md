# Aptitude Test Preparation Guide

(Fictional sample guide written for the KRIT placement cell.)

## Why it matters

The aptitude test is the first filter in most drives, and in mass recruiters it removes more than half the applicants. It is also the most predictable round: the same question types appear every year.

## The three sections

**Quantitative aptitude.** Percentages, ratio and proportion, profit and loss, simple and compound interest, time and work, time speed and distance, averages, permutations and combinations, and basic probability.

**Logical reasoning.** Number and letter series, seating arrangements, blood relations, directions, syllogisms, coding and decoding, and data sufficiency.

**Verbal ability.** Reading comprehension, sentence correction, fill in the blanks, synonyms and antonyms, and para jumbles.

Analytics companies add **data interpretation**: questions based on tables, bar charts and pie charts.

## Four-week plan

- **Week 1:** Percentages, ratios, profit and loss, averages. 30 questions a day.
- **Week 2:** Time and work, time speed and distance, interest. 30 questions a day.
- **Week 3:** Logical reasoning, one puzzle type per day. Start one reading passage a day.
- **Week 4:** Full-length timed tests on alternate days. Review every wrong answer on the days between.

## Time management

- Most tests give about one minute per question. If a question takes more than 90 seconds, mark it and move on.
- Check whether there is negative marking before you start. If there is none, attempt every question.
- Where there is a sectional cut-off, give each section its planned time even if another section feels easier.
- In data interpretation, read all the questions of a set before calculating. Often two answers come from one calculation.

## Shortcuts worth learning

- Percentage to fraction conversions up to one twelfth.
- Squares up to 30 and cubes up to 15.
- The LCM method for time and work problems.
- Relative speed for trains and boats.

## Common mistakes

- Practising without a timer.
- Leaving verbal ability for the last week.
- Guessing in tests with negative marking.
- Not attending the placement cell's training sessions, which are compulsory and count towards the 75 percent attendance requirement.
